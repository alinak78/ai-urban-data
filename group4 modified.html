The big picture

An accessible journey depends on more than a train arriving. Elevators and escalators connect the street, the station, and the platform—and their reliability matters.

The MTA NYCT Subway Elevator and Escalator Availability dataset records the percentage of time this equipment is operational. Published through New York State Open Data, it offers a monthly view of performance beginning in 2015.

Each record represents one elevator or escalator in one reporting month, with station and borough details alongside availability, outage counts, and other operational measures.

Reading the numbers

Availability describes the share of reported equipment hours during which an asset was operational. A higher percentage means a greater share of that period was available.

Hours availableTotal reported hours× 100=Availability %
TIME AVAILABLE, IN CONTEXT

To combine records, divide the sum of available hours by the sum of total reported hours. This weights the result by time, so equipment with different reported hours contributes proportionally. Exclude records without valid total hours.

Availability and outage counts answer different questions: one long outage can affect availability more than several short interruptions. An equipment record also describes a single asset; it does not establish whether a complete station journey is accessible.

A few useful things to know

Is this a live service-status dataset?

These are monthly historical performance records. For current equipment outages and travel information, visit MTA elevator and escalator status.

Where can I find the full documentation?

The original dataset page includes an overview and a data dictionary published by the MTA. Consult those documents for field definitions and reporting details.

Who publishes the data?

The Metropolitan Transportation Authority publishes this dataset for New York City Transit through New York State Open Data. For dataset questions, contact OpenData@mtahq.org.

Is NYC Access an official MTA website?

NYC Access is an independent explanation of a public dataset. The MTA and New York State Open Data remain the authoritative sources for the records and their documentation.
